Planetary protection is a guiding principle in the design of an interplanetary mission, aiming to prevent biological contamination of both the target celestial body and the Earth in the case of sample-return missions. Planetary protection reflects both the unknown nature of the space environment and the desire of the scientific community to preserve the pristine nature of celestial bodies until they can be studied in detail.
There are two types of interplanetary contamination. Forward contamination is the transfer of viable organisms from Earth to another celestial body. Back contamination is the transfer of potential extraterrestrial organisms back to the Earth's biosphere. Sterilizing human missions is impossible, as the microbiome of a single human typically hosts a hundred trillion (1014) microorganisms of thousands of species, which cannot be removed without killing the person.
