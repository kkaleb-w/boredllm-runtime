Welfare biology is a proposed interdisciplinary field concerned with the welfare of living beings in relation to their environment. The concept was introduced by the economist Yew-Kwang Ng in 1995, when he defined welfare as the balance of enjoyment and suffering experienced by an organism. His proposal combined welfare economics with evolutionary biology to examine how natural processes affect the welfare of sentient individuals.
Welfare biology has subsequently been discussed in animal ethics, animal welfare science, ecology, and research on wild animal suffering. Its proposed scope includes the welfare of animals living outside direct human control, whose experiences have received comparatively little attention in conventional animal welfare research. Suggested research areas include population dynamics, life history, habitat conditions, and the effects of environmental changes on individual animals. Some researchers advocate establishing welfare biology as a separate discipline, while others favour extending existing animal welfare science to include wild animals.
